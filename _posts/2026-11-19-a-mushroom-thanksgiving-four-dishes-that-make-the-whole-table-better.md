I'll admit something - I'm not a huge fan of Turkey! 

As a southerner, we always had ham and turkey. I always gravitated towards the ham and used the turkey for the left overs. 

Well this is a mushroom themed blog I am going to advocate that you can incorporate mushrooms throughout the menu. 

Hey, bring some earthy and buttery mushroom goodness to that bland and gray food! And the added Tryptophan might knock out your annoying uncle that drank too much.

The dressing. The gravy. The green bean casserole. Now the turkey is the supporting act instead of the main dish. 

Mushrooms are an unfair advantage on a holiday table. They're savory, they soak up butter and herbs, and they're made for the kind of cooking that happens the day before. Four dishes, and almost all of it can be done ahead, so you aren't stuck in the kitchen while everyone else is eating.

What you'll need: about 3.5 lbs of mushrooms. Cremini are my friends for this. Add shiitake wherever you want depth, and oyster or maitake if your local farm has them. Buy them a couple of days early and keep them in a paper bag in the fridge.

# The Game Plan

2 days before: make the marinated mushrooms. Dry the bread cubes for the dressing. The day before: make the cream of mushroom soup, the gravy, and assemble the dressing and the green beans (don't bake).Thanksgiving: bake, reheat, serve. Boom! That's the whole day.

# 1. Marinated Mushrooms (The Appetizer)

Put these out while people are still taking off their coats. They get better the longer they sit, and they cost almost nothing. A toothpick, a small bowl, and a bit of bread is all you need.

Serves: 8 to 10 as a nibble

Time: 15 minutes, plus at least 4 hours of marinating

## Ingredients

1 lb small cremini or white button mushrooms, halved if larger than a walnut2 tbsp olive oil, for the pan½ cup olive oil¼ cup red wine vinegar2 tbsp lemon juice3 garlic cloves, smashed1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves1 tsp Dijon mustard1 tsp sugar1 tsp kosher salt½ tsp black pepper. Pinch of red pepper flakes1 bay leaf. 

## Method

Heat the 2 tbsp olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms in a single layer (cook in two batches if you need to) and leave them alone for 3 minutes. Stir and cook until browned, about 6 to 8 minutes total. While they cook, whisk everything else together in a bowl: the ½ cup olive oil, vinegar, lemon juice, garlic, thyme, mustard, sugar, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes. Drop in the bay leaf. Pour the hot mushrooms into the marinade and stir. Warm mushrooms soak it up much better than cold ones. Cover and refrigerate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. Stir once or twice if you remember. Take them out an hour before serving so they're not ice cold. Fish out the bay leaf and the garlic. They keep about 5 days in the fridge.

# 2. Mushroom Sourdough Dressing

Sourdough does something regular bread can't. It stays chewy in the middle and crisp on top, and the tang keeps all that butter from feeling heavy. The mushrooms are what make people ask what's in it.

Serves: 8 to 10

Time: 30 minutes of prep, 50 minutes in the oven, plus drying the bread

## Ingredients

1 loaf sourdough (about 1¼ lb), cut into 1-inch cubes, about 12 cups1½ lb mixed mushrooms, roughly chopped3 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp olive oil, for the mushrooms3 tbsp butter, plus more to grease the dish1 large yellow onion, diced3 celery ribs, diced4 garlic cloves, minced1 tbsp fresh sage, chopped1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves1 tsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped½ cup dry white wine (or dry sherry)2½ cups chicken or mushroom stock, warmed2 large eggs, beaten½ cup fresh parsley, chopped1½ tsp kosher salt, plus more for the mushrooms½ tsp black pepper

## Method

Dry out the bread. Spread the cubes on two baking sheets and leave them out overnight, or bake at 300°F for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring once, until dry and lightly toasted. Fresh bread turns to mush. Brown the mushrooms. Heat half the mushroom butter and oil in your largest skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the mushrooms with a pinch of salt and don't touch them for 3 minutes. Stir and cook until deeply browned and the liquid is gone, about 8 minutes. Scoop into a big bowl and repeat with the rest. Crowd the pan and they steam instead of brown. Soften the vegetables. Melt the 3 tbsp butter in the same pan. Add the onion and celery with a pinch of salt and cook about 8 minutes until soft and golden. Add the garlic, sage, thyme and rosemary and cook another minute. Deglaze. Pour in the wine and scrape up the brown bits. Let it bubble until almost gone, about 2 minutes. Combine. Add the vegetables to the bowl with the mushrooms. Add the bread, parsley, salt and pepper and toss gently. Add the liquid. Whisk the eggs into the warm stock and pour it over the bread a little at a time, tossing as you go. It should look moist but not swimming. Bake. Spoon it into a buttered 9x13 dish without packing it down. Cover with foil and bake at 375°F for 25 minutes. Uncover and bake 25 minutes more, until deep golden and crunchy on top. Rest 10 minutes before serving.

Make ahead: assemble through step 6, cover, and refrigerate up to a day. Add 5 to 10 minutes to the covered bake.

# 3. Mushroom Gravy

This is the one people will ask you about. It's deep and savory, it works for vegetarians if you use mushroom stock, and it has a big advantage: you can make it days ahead, so you never face a panicked pan of drippings while the turkey rests.

Makes: about 3½ cups

Time: 35 minutes

## Ingredients

4 tbsp butter8 oz mushrooms (cremini, with a few shiitake if you have them), very finely chopped1 shallot, finely chopped2 garlic cloves, minced¼ cup all-purpose flour3 cups warm turkey, chicken or mushroom stock1 tbsp soy sauce1 tbsp dry sherry or Madeira (optional, but good)1 tsp fresh thyme leaves. Salt and black pepper. More pepper :) 

## Method

Melt the butter in a large saucepan over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until deeply browned. They'll give off liquid first. Be patient and let it cook off. Add the shallot and garlic and cook 2 minutes. Sprinkle the flour over the top and stir for 2 minutes. It should smell a little nutty. Pour in the sherry, if using, and stir until it's mostly gone. Add the stock a little at a time, whisking after each addition to keep it smooth. Add the soy sauce and thyme. Simmer over medium-low heat for 12 to 15 minutes until it coats a spoon. Taste and add salt and pepper. For a silky gravy, blend it with an immersion blender. For a rustic one, leave the mushrooms in pieces. Both are good.

Make ahead: it keeps 4 days in the fridge and thickens as it sits. Reheat with a splash of stock. On the day, whisk in a few spoonfuls of turkey drippings for extra flavor.

# 4. Green Bean Casserole with Homemade Cream of Mushroom

Here's where I throw out the can. The original casserole is a classic for a reason, but real mushrooms, butter and cream make it taste like what it was always supposed to be. The sauce takes 25 minutes. Keep the crispy onions on top. I'm not a monster.

Serves: 8

Time: 1 hour, most of it hands-off

The Homemade Cream of Mushroom

Makes: about 2 cups

## Ingredients

3 tbsp butter12 oz cremini mushrooms, very finely chopped (a food processor makes this quick)1 shallot, minced2 garlic cloves, minced3 tbsp all-purpose flour1 cup chicken or mushroom stock1 cup half-and-half1 tsp soy sauce½ tsp fresh thyme leaves¾ tsp kosher salt, ¼ tsp black pepper

## Method

Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms and cook 8 to 10 minutes until browned and dry. Add the shallot and garlic and cook 2 minutes. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute. Whisk in the stock, then the half-and-half. Add the soy sauce, thyme, salt and pepper. Simmer 5 to 8 minutes, whisking, until very thick, like a condensed soup. This is thicker than soup you'd eat from a bowl, and that's on purpose.

# The Green Bean Casserole

## Ingredients

1½ lb green beans (use French cut), trimmed and cut into 2-inch pieces2 cups homemade cream of mushroom (above)1½ cups crispy fried onions (store-bought is fine, or fry your own shallots)Salt, for the blanching water

## Method

Bring a big pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook the green beans 3 minutes, then drain and plunge them into ice water. This keeps them bright green. Drain well and pat dry. Toss the beans with the cream of mushroom and ½ cup of the crispy onions. Spoon into a 2-quart baking dish. Bake at 375°F for 25 minutes, until bubbling at the edges. Scatter the remaining 1 cup onions on top and bake 5 to 8 minutes more until golden.

Make ahead: make the soup up to 2 days ahead and the blanched beans the day before. Combine and bake on Thanksgiving. Hold off on the onions until the last few minutes.

## The Shopping List, Roughly

Mushrooms: about 3¾ lb total (1 lb marinated, 1½ lb dressing, ½ lb gravy, ¾ lb soup) Produce: 1 yellow onion, 3 celery ribs, 3 shallots, a head and a half of garlic, 1½ lb green beans, 2 lemons, parsley Herbs: fresh thyme (lots), sage, rosemary, 1 bay leaf Dairy and eggs: about 1½ sticks butter plus extra, 1 cup half-and-half, 2 eggs Pantry: 1 loaf sourdough, 6 to 7 cups stock, flour, olive oil, red wine vinegar, Dijon, soy sauce, white wine, dry sherry, crispy fried onions

It's a lot of mushrooms. I think that's a good problem. Buy local ones if you can.
